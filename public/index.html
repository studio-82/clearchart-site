Where the paper chart lets residents down

Ask anyone who has done a medication round and you'll hear the same four stories. None of them are about careless people. They're about a paper folder trying to keep track of every change, for every resident, across every shift.

On paper With Clearchart

The script was written, but the chart wasn't

On paper

Rx Cefalexin 500mg QID 5/7chart: nothing written

During a visit the GP writes a new prescription but doesn't update the chart. The pharmacy supplies one thing. Staff give another.

With Clearchart

The doctor updates the chart on the tablet during the visit. Before they sign off, Clearchart asks whether they've written any scripts, so each one goes on the chart before anyone leaves the room.

Nobody can read what the doctor wrote

On paper

Frusemide 40mg mane

Is that 40 or 10? Morning or night? Staff ring the surgery, wait for a call back, or make their best guess.

With Clearchart

The doctor picks the medicine from a list of standard Australian medicine names, then enters the dose, route and timing in their own fields. Nothing is handwritten.

The change nobody noticed

On paper

Metoprolol 50mg 25mg BD

A dose is crossed out and rewritten halfway down the chart. The next shift works from memory and gives the old dose.

With Clearchart

Changed orders stay highlighted for every staff member until they acknowledge them. Clinical leads can see who hasn't.

Copying everything onto a new chart

On paper

Resident health information stays in Australia, and stays yours

Clearchart isn't live yet. These are the commitments we're designing against now, so you can hold us to them at launch.

Backed up in Australia
Each chart is backed up to Australian servers whenever the tablet is online. Backups and logs never leave the country.
Built for Australian privacy law
Designed around the Australian Privacy Principles, the Notifiable Data Breaches scheme and the health records laws in New South Wales, Victoria and the ACT.
Your organisation owns the records
Export everything whenever you like. If you leave, we delete your data on a published schedule and confirm it in writing.
Every view is logged, not just every edit
Clinical leads can see who opened a resident's chart, and when.
Only what's needed
We keep what staff need to give medicines safely, not full clinical notes. The less we hold, the less there is to lose.
A lost tablet isn't a lost chart
Every tablet is encrypted and locked with a PIN. If one goes missing, wipe it remotely and carry on from another. Nothing is lost.

We will never sell resident information, use it for advertising, or use it to train AI models.

Questions

Does the doctor still need to write a prescription?

Yes. Clearchart replaces the paper medication chart, not the prescription. Doctors keep writing scripts the way they do now, and Clearchart asks them to add every script to the chart before they sign off the visit.

Does the doctor need an account?

No. Doctors use your facility's tablet, the same way they use the paper chart now, and sign each change on the screen.

Can we take it to the pharmacy?

Yes. Take the tablet to the pharmacy just as you would the paper chart, or print an up-to-date copy.

Does it need internet?

No. The chart lives on the tablet, so rounds and GP visits work anywhere. It backs up to Australian servers the next time the tablet is online.

What if a tablet goes missing?

It's encrypted and PIN-locked, so nobody can read it. Wipe it remotely, sign in on another tablet, and the chart is there from the last backup.

What do we need to use it?

A tablet for each medication chart folder you use now. Clearchart runs on standard iPads and Android tablets.

Is it only for residential aged care?

We're starting with residential aged care. Disability accommodation, respite and day programs use similar charts, so tell us if that's you.

What will it cost?

We're setting pricing with our first facilities. Waitlist members will see it first and can help shape it.
